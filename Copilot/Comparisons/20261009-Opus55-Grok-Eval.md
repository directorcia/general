## Comparative Analysis

### Executive Summary

- **Strongest overall:** Document A — superior methodological transparency, evidence discipline and model disclosure.
- **Strongest argument:** Document A — more systematic testing of the thesis and clearer falsification criteria.
- **Best evidence discipline:** Document A — tiered sources, evidence ledger, independent verification and full reconstruction of market multiples.
- **Greatest depth:** Document A — fuller model assumptions register, cohort analysis and sensitivity results.
- **Most polished presentation:** Document A edges Document B on structure and navigation aids; Document B is slightly more concise and immediately readable for a busy owner.

### Head-to-Head Comparison

**Document A does better than Document B on:**
- Explicit design to falsify the obsolescence thesis.
- Evidence grading, ledger and post-draft audit.
- Full disclosure of every model input and sensitivity results.
- Separation of normal business churn from AI-driven outcomes.
- Formal signposts that would change the conclusions.

**Document A falls short relative to Document B on:**
- Length and density; some executives may prefer Document B’s tighter narrative.
- Immediate “bottom-line” punch in the opening paragraph.

**Document B does better than Document A on:**
- Conciseness and executive readability in the first 5–10 pages.
- Memorable framing of the core economic problem (“labour-priced core is losing its foundation”).
- Clean service-line vulnerability table.

**Document B falls short relative to Document A on:**
- Depth of evidence verification and model transparency.
- Formal treatment of uncertainty and falsifiability.
- Population-level (cohort) analysis.

**Materiality of weaknesses**
- Document A’s length is a minor presentation issue, not a substance problem.
- Document B’s thinner verification and less transparent modelling are material for any reader who intends to rely on the quantitative conclusions or re-run the model with their own numbers.

### Critical Findings

**Unsupported or weakly supported claims**
- Both documents correctly flag vendor-sponsored surveys and secondary sources. Document A is more systematic about it.
- Document B contains a handful of high-impact AI-resolution and M&A-multiple figures that rest on single secondary or vendor sources; Document A’s ledger and audit process reduce this risk.

**Citation discipline**
- Document A is exemplary (tiers, secondary flags, reconstruction of every public-market input).
- Document B is good but less exhaustive.

**Logical consistency**
- Both reach the same high-level conclusion (industry survives; traditional labour-priced formula is under structural pressure). Differences are in timing, magnitude and degree of transparency.
- No material internal contradictions detected in either report.

**Verbosity**
- Document A is long but purposeful; almost every page adds evidence, model logic or a caveat.
- Document B is tighter; a few sections could still be pruned without loss.

**Missing context**
- Both correctly note the absence of public small-business price and churn data.
- Document A is more explicit about geographic weighting and the illustrative nature of the archetype.

**Presentation issues**
- Document A: occasional information-dense tables.
- Document B: fewer navigation aids for a long report.

---

## Final Verdict

Document A deserves the top rank because it treats the obsolescence claim as a hypothesis to be tested, subjects its own evidence to an independent audit, fully discloses every modelling assumption, and supplies the reader with concrete signposts that would overturn its conclusions. These features materially increase the report’s credibility and usefulness for a sceptical owner or investor.

For Document B to surpass Document A it would need:
1. An explicit evidence ledger and post-draft claim audit of comparable scope.
2. A fully registered assumptions table (sourced / derived / judgement) with sensitivity results.
3. Clearer separation of normal business churn from AI-driven outcomes.
4. Formal, measurable signposts that would change the conclusions.

Until those elements are present, Document A remains the stronger, more defensible strategic research product.
