## Comparative Analysis

### Executive Summary

- **Strongest overall:** Document B (Astra). It combines the same strategic conclusion with tighter methodological controls, deeper financial transparency and more consistent evidence hygiene.  
- **Strongest argument:** Document B — clearer separation of observation from inference and more systematic treatment of counter-arguments and falsifiers.  
- **Best evidence discipline:** Document B — denser, better-bounded citations and active exclusion of unsupported claims.  
- **Greatest depth:** Document B — fuller equations, sensitivities, historical analogies and commercial architecture.  
- **Most polished presentation:** Effectively tied (both 8.5). Document A has a slightly more executive-friendly, punchy tone; Document B is denser but equally professional.

### Head-to-Head Comparison

**Document B (Astra) vs Document A (Sol)**  
- **What B does better:** Evidence labelling, financial-model transparency (equations + isolated sensitivities), explicit low-confidence framing of population judgements, richer historical and Australian context, more systematic falsification language.  
- **Where B falls short:** Slightly denser prose and an earlier cut-off date; pure executive readability is fractionally lower.  
- **Materiality of weaknesses:** Minor. The denser style does not obscure the argument; the cut-off difference is small given the shared source universe.

**Document A (Sol) vs Document B (Astra)**  
- **What A does better:** More concise executive summary and “Key Findings” tables; marginally later research horizon; slightly more immediately board-scannable visual hierarchy in places.  
- **Where A falls short:** Less granular evidence boundaries, thinner source register, financial model that discloses inputs but not the full algebraic machinery, less explicit warning language around the 2035 cohort table.  
- **Materiality of weaknesses:** Moderate on evidence discipline and financial transparency; minor on presentation and argument clarity. These gaps prevent A from overtaking B when scores are close.

### Critical Findings

**Unsupported or weakly supported claims**  
- Neither document asserts a precise industry failure rate as fact. Both correctly treat the 2035/2036 cohort tables as planning judgements. B is more explicit about this limitation.  
- Both rely on Service Leadership panel data with appropriate caveats; A’s citation of the panel is accurate but less repeatedly bounded than B’s.

**Citation concerns**  
- A uses a clean numbered list [1]–[45]; B uses a richer coded system (R01–R73 + M1) with more boundary notes. Both are consistent internally. No broken or fabricated citations were identified relative to the public sources available at the respective cut-offs.  
- Secondary partner summaries (Pax8 in A; IDC partner study in B) are flagged as such in both.

**Logical consistency**  
- Both maintain internal consistency between the bifurcation thesis, the financial stress cases and the recommended actions. No material contradictions detected.

**Verbosity**  
- Neither is unnecessarily verbose. B is denser in the financial and evidence sections; the extra length adds analytical value (equations, sensitivities, historical tables). A is more economical in the executive layers.

**Missing context / analysis**  
- A could strengthen its evidence-register and financial-equation transparency.  
- B could lighten a few dense tables for pure executive scanning and, if possible, incorporate any material signals between 17–23 September.

**Presentation issues**  
- Both are high-quality professional documents. No significant formatting defects. A’s visual hierarchy is fractionally more “board-pack” ready; B’s is more “research-report” dense.

---

## Final Verdict

**Document B (Astra) deserves the top ranking** because it delivers the same strategic conclusion with measurably superior evidence discipline, a fully reproducible financial model, and more rigorous separation of observation from judgement. These advantages outweigh Document A’s modest edge in executive concision and slightly later cut-off.

**To surpass B, Document A would need to:**  
1. Adopt an explicit evidence-labelling system (Observed / Capability / Forecast / Inference / Scenario) and apply it consistently.  
2. Publish the full algebraic equations and isolated price-versus-productivity sensitivities of its financial model.  
3. Strengthen the low-confidence framing around any population or failure-rate planning tables.  
4. Expand the source register with the same level of boundary notes currently present in B.

Both documents are high-calibre strategic research products. The ranking reflects relative, not absolute, quality; either would serve a board or investor well, with B providing the stronger foundation for decision underwriting.
