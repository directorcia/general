## Comparative Analysis

### Executive Summary

- **Strongest overall:** Document A — superior depth, transparent multi-scenario modelling, graded evidence infrastructure and falsifiable signposts make it the more complete decision tool for an owner or board.
- **Strongest argument:** Document A — clearer multi-question structure, explicit thesis-testing design and better integration of counter-arguments with measurable thresholds.
- **Best evidence discipline:** Document A (narrowly) — four-tier grading, 45-claim ledger and independent verification edge out Document B’s excellent caution and labelling system.
- **Greatest depth:** Document A — full assumptions register, sensitivity matrix, cohort model and 401-source base.
- **Most polished presentation:** Document B is more readable and less overwhelming for a first pass; Document A is more professionally complete once the reader invests the time.

### Head-to-Head Comparison

**Document A does better than Document B at:**
- Systematic coverage of every research question with graded strength ratings.
- Building and stress-testing a multi-path financial and cohort model.
- Providing an auditable evidence ledger and verification trail.
- Giving concrete, sequenced recommendations and board metrics.
- Quantifying (with declared judgement) exposure rankings and population outcomes.

**Document A falls short relative to Document B in:**
- Executive accessibility and length discipline (material for time-constrained readers).
- Occasional numerical precision on pure-judgement inputs (scenario weights, some population shares).
- Slightly higher residual risk of causal wording that exceeds the source (largely corrected).

**Document B does better than Document A at:**
- Maintaining strict boundaries on what the evidence can and cannot support.
- Avoiding any invented industry failure rate or universal multiple.
- Presenting a cleaner, shorter narrative that is easier to brief.
- Isolating the pure price-versus-productivity mechanism with a simple sensitivity table.

**Document B falls short relative to Document A in:**
- Depth of Microsoft programme economics, security-line analysis and long-horizon modelling (material).
- Absence of an independent verification step or formal evidence ledger (material for high-stakes use).
- Less complete transformation sequencing and board-level measurement framework (moderate).

Weaknesses of Document A are mostly presentational and second-order numerical; weaknesses of Document B are substantive gaps in coverage and modelling completeness.

### Critical Findings

**Unsupported or weakly supported claims**
- Document A: Level-1 end-to-end resolution ranges and scenario weights are openly labelled judgement yet still carry high numerical specificity.
- Document B: Starting EBITDA margin of 16.5 % is an author choice above the public panel average; the text acknowledges this but the base case remains optimistic.

**Citation discipline**
- Document A is stronger on volume, tiering and verification; a minority of 2026 market-data points rest on aggregator pages (mitigated by Appendix D).
- Document B is stronger on refusal to cite what cannot be verified; fewer primary sources overall.

**Logical consistency**
- Both documents are internally consistent. Document A’s verification pass corrected earlier causal over-statements. Document B’s caution sometimes under-weights the cumulative effect of platform leakage + price pressure.

**Verbosity**
- Document A is long; some tables and appendices could be moved to an online supplement without loss of argument.
- Document B is appropriately concise; a few tables lack sufficient surrounding explanation.

**Missing context / analysis**
- Document A: limited Australian primary data.
- Document B: thinner treatment of FY27 partner-incentive changes, security commoditisation price points, and public-market valuation signals.

**Presentation issues**
- Document A: dense multi-column tables and very long sections reduce skim-ability.
- Document B: fewer navigational aids and cross-references.

## Final Verdict

Document A deserves the top rank because it converts the same research brief into a more complete, transparent and falsifiable decision instrument. Its evidence infrastructure (ledger + verification), multi-path financial model with full assumptions register, graded findings table, exposure rankings and eight measurable signposts give an owner or board concrete tools that Document B, for all its admirable caution, simply does not supply at the same level of depth or operational utility.

To surpass Document A, Document B would need:
1. A comparably transparent multi-scenario model with sensitivity analysis and an explicit assumptions register.
2. A graded evidence ledger (or equivalent) covering the core claims.
3. Fuller treatment of Microsoft partner economics, security-line pricing and public-market signals.
4. A more developed transformation sequence and board scorecard.

Conversely, Document A would become even stronger by:
1. Producing a 8–12 page executive briefing version that preserves the key tables and signposts.
2. Moving pure-judgement numerical weights into clearly labelled ranges rather than point estimates.
3. Adding a short Australian/ANZ data appendix.

Both documents correctly reject blanket obsolescence and correctly identify the narrower thesis (labour-priced, undifferentiated services are being repriced and bundled). Document A simply equips the reader more thoroughly to act on that conclusion.
