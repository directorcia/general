## Head-to-Head Comparison

**Document A vs Document B**  
Document A does better on argumentative discipline, source transparency, and executive readability. Its weaker relative areas (slightly less granular labour statistics, less detail on certain FY27 mechanics) are minor and do not undermine the overall analysis.  

Document B does better on raw specificity in talent, buyer surveys, and some valuation data. Its main shortfalls relative to A are less systematic evidence grading and higher density that reduces polish. These weaknesses are material for an executive audience but not fatal to the underlying analysis.  

Both reports correctly identify the same core dynamics (labour exposure, platform disintermediation, security migration, valuation bifurcation) and reach compatible strategic conclusions. Differences are primarily in calibration, transparency, and presentation rather than fundamental disagreement.

---

## Critical Findings

- **Unsupported or weakly supported claims:** Both reports carefully caveate vendor AI resolution rates. Document B leans slightly more heavily on secondary aggregators for some operational benchmarks without equivalent limitation language.  
- **Citation concerns:** Document A’s grading system is more rigorous. Document B’s citations are specific and generally appropriate but less consistently tiered.  
- **Logical issues:** None material in either document. Both maintain internal consistency.  
- **Verbosity:** Document B is denser in places; some historical and survey detail could be tightened without loss of force. Document A is better calibrated.  
- **Missing context:** Both acknowledge Australian/New Zealand data limitations. Neither has access to full paywalled Service Leadership or IDC reports (explicitly noted).  
- **Presentation issues:** Document B’s higher information density reduces scannability. Document A’s textual figure descriptions are a format constraint rather than a content flaw.

---

## Final Verdict

**Document A (November 2026) ranks first** because it more rigorously treats the brief as a hypothesis test, maintains clearer separation between sourced evidence and author modelling, and delivers higher executive polish without sacrificing analytical depth. Its probability-weighted scenarios, explicit source grading, and calibrated conclusions give a sceptical owner greater confidence in the reasoning process itself.

For Document B to surpass it, it would need:  
1. A systematic source-grading system equivalent to Document A’s Primary/Secondary/Vendor tiers.  
2. More explicit and consistent labelling of every author assumption and modelling choice.  
3. Tighter editing to reduce density and improve scannability for a time-constrained owner.  
4. Slightly more cautious calibration of conclusions where evidence rests primarily on vendor or secondary sources.

Both reports are high-quality independent analyses of the same question. Document A’s edge lies in methodological transparency and presentation discipline rather than superior insight into the underlying industry dynamics.
