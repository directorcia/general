## Comparative Analysis

### Executive Summary
- **Strongest overall:** Document A. Tightest argument discipline, best evidence boundaries and most immediately usable capital gates.  
- **Strongest argument:** Document A (narrow edge over C).  
- **Best evidence discipline:** Document A (narrow edge over C; both substantially ahead of B).  
- **Greatest depth:** Document B (market/security/historical breadth).  
- **Most polished / executive-friendly presentation:** Document A.  

### Head-to-Head Comparisons

**A versus C**  
A is slightly tighter on gates, inference language and executive path. C is stronger on cash-trough quantification, pricing-headroom sensitivity and residual multi-vendor framing, and uses a larger reference firm. Differences are material for a pure board paper but small overall.  

**A versus B**  
A is clearly superior on Argument and Evidence. B’s extra breadth does not compensate for looser claim calibration.  

**C versus B**  
C is superior on Argument and Evidence (decision quality). B is superior on raw Detail breadth. C’s financial and residual-service analysis is more decision-useful.  

**Weaknesses materiality**  
- A’s narrower market coverage: minor for its stated purpose.  
- C’s title–body mismatch and slightly softer gates: minor-to-moderate.  
- B’s assertive magnitude/probability claims: material relative to the other two when used as a decision paper.

### Critical Findings
- **Unsupported or weakly bounded claims:** B’s 30–60% labour-removal and precise industry-fate percentages carry more narrative weight than A or C allow for their own scenarios. All three correctly label cohort exercises as hypothetical.  
- **Citation quality:** All strong. A and C are tighter on inference boundaries; B’s grading system is a compensating strength.  
- **Logical consistency:** All three correctly note that current profitability coexists with future task-level price pressure. No material internal contradictions.  
- **Verbosity:** B is longest; some descriptive sections exceed incremental decision value. A and C are more concentrated.  
- **Missing context:** A and C under-weight broad historical consolidation patterns relative to B. B under-weights the quantified “Continue can still win on cash” case that both A and C emphasise.  
- **Presentation:** A is the most board-ready. C’s title is the only notable polish issue.  

---

## Final Verdict

Document A remains the top-ranked report because it best combines rigorous evidence boundaries, a fully reconciled and assumption-transparent financial model, and practical, reversible capital gates. It is the most reliable board paper of the three.

Document C is a very close second and would surpass A with:  
1. A title that matches the cautious body;  
2. Slightly tighter tranche limits and evidence conditions comparable to A’s; and  
3. An even more explicit executive reading path.  

Its cash-trough, pricing-sensitivity and residual-service analysis are already distinctive strengths that an owner or investor would value.

Document B would need to tighten calibration of large labour-displacement and probability claims, add a fully reconciled conventional-alternative cash analysis, and increase decision density to overtake either A or C.

All three are high-quality professional work. The ranking reflects relative superiority on the weighted criteria for an owner/investor decision use-case.
